## Method used for every row
1. **Discovery:** city-by-city searches with reproducible query URLs. Two query shapes were used and they are not interchangeable: `yelp.com/search?find_desc=Lunch+Specials&find_loc=<city>` pages give *names and reviewer quotes but no addresses* (discovery only), while the multi-business capture query `yelp <City> hours address "Name1" "Name2" "Name3" "Name4"` returns two or three **complete** business captures per search - per-day hours, street address, phone, price band and website. Roughly 45 searches and 6 page fetches were run; the multi-business shape is what made 31 rows possible in one pass.
2. **Screening before verification:** candidates were deduped against `data/lunch_specials.json` and `data/lunch_rejected.json` on normalized name+city *and* street address before any search budget was spent. This step was introduced mid-pass (see LUNCH-FLAG-103) after `batch16b` turned out to be eight duplicates.
3. **Resolution:** each row had to resolve to one street address and a per-day hours block from a page that can be cited. Where sources disagreed, the disagreement is stored in `days_open`, `hours_tuesday` and `flags` rather than averaged away. Coordinates are recorded only where a source actually printed them (roadtrippers, corner.inc, TripAdvisor, Yahoo Local and the restaurants' own JSON-LD).
4. **Price rule:** a price is printed only when it appears on the business' own page (site, ordering menu or published PDF). Reviewer-quoted figures - Kiya Sushi's $14.99 trio roll, Country Way's $7.95 bacon burger, Jang Su Jang's $75 galbi jjim, Elia's $29 moussaka, Tai Pan's "5 for $1" sesame balls - are attached to the row as quotes and deliberately kept out of `price_from`/`price_to`. Every one of the 31 new rows therefore carries no price, which is the honest state of the evidence, not a gap in the work.
5. **Absence is a result:** "dinner only", "Tuesday closed", "closes at 1:00 PM" and "dessert venue, no lunch menu" are recorded as findings so the gaps are visible (see the no-lunch list above).
6. **Nothing was backfilled by hand:** the merge script only accepted rows whose `verification.sources` list was non-empty and whose name and street address did not already exist in the master list.

## Source-access limitations recorded for the next pass
- **Yelp returns HTTP 403 to direct fetches** from this environment, so every Yelp citation in this pass is a *dated search capture* and the row's verification level says so (`listing`/`review`, never `official` on Yelp alone).
- **Bash has no outbound network** (TLS connect failure), so all research ran through the web tools; nothing could be scraped in bulk.
- Dead sources confirmed again: `zmenu.com` city pages (HTTP 500), `menupix.com` city pages (404), `toasttab.com/local/order/<city>` (redirects to a 404), OpenTable landmark pages (404), and `allmenus.com` "Lunch Specials" category pages (1-5 results per city - not a bulk source). `restaurantji.com` city pages are JS-paginated into ~23 chunks, so only its per-business snippet data is usable.
- Proxy/cache-routed URLs (`routify-file-proxy-*.aliyuncs.com`, which returned SignatureDoesNotMatch) must never be cited; `check_links.py` bans those hosts and the 2,176 URLs in the data all pass.
- **Name traps found this pass:** three different "Los Altos" restaurants outside the Bay Area (Salinas, Goleta, San Bernardino), a Saratoga Springs **NY** Restaurant Week page, and the Das Bierhauz / Bierhaus pair two blocks apart on Castro St where only one is open.
